This pack contains two sailing systems. Small Ships supplies complete vessels with seats, cargo, wind and naval guns. Eureka with Valkyrien Skies turns a connected block build into a moving, walkable ship. Neither requires creative mode. The title artwork depicts the Thousand Sunny; an exact Sunny or Merry build is not included as a ready-made schematic.

A large ship in Survival

Complete Your first command in /voyage: deliver 32 oak planks, 12 white wool and 4 iron ingots for an Oak Cog. This introductory contract deliberately subsidizes your first vessel.

Then collect 336 oak planks, 72 white wool, 36 string and 12 iron ingots. Use /voyage claim grand_galleon to exchange them for the native Oak Galleon. This one-time contract requires the first shipwright contract and gives 20 supply credits. Carry everything together; the pack consumes exact materials. Place the resulting ship item in a broad, deep stretch of water.

The Galleon has three masts and ten cannon positions. The smaller two-masted Brigg has six. These are working ship entities, not scenery. The integration test verifies that the Galleon item is delivered and its entity can instantiate. Actual sailing and multiplayer deck behavior still need the Windows playtest.

Build, equip and repair at a Dockyard

JEI's @smallships search shows the Dockyard, cannon and ammunition recipes. Build the Dockyard beside a clear harbor. Its ship selector checks launch room and depth, lists materials, and takes them from your inventory. There is no ordinary crafting-table recipe for these finished ships.

The pinned 2.0.1 JAR's native dockyard recipes specify:

Native recipes accept plank tags; the custom Galleon contract asks for oak. Times assume 20 server ticks per second. The Dockyard can repeatedly build replacement ships and offers repairs, upgrades, naming and cannon fitting. Cannons are separate equipment. Buy them and cannonballs with /voyage shop, or craft them through JEI; place ammunition in the ship inventory.

Have the captain board first, then let friends take seats or guns. Reduce sail before approaching port. Wind, collisions and damaged sails affect handling. A sunk ship is lost, so keep valuables ashore before your first combat voyage. The author's Small Ships guide describes the naval systems. Controls, recipes and names were also inspected in the pinned mod's language and recipe resources rather than assumed from its latest release.

Build a ship you can live aboard

Make a small wooden hull in water first, with a deck and clear separation from land, temporary scaffolding and the dock. Add an Oak Ship Helm, found under @vs_eureka in JEI. Sneak-right-click the helm and choose Assemble. Right-click the assembled helm to pilot. Check Eureka's bindings in the Controls screen.

Once that works, expand your design with a cabin, storage, masts and sails. Use Eureka floaters when needed to support a heavy hull. Its anchor and ballast blocks offer further control. Connected hull blocks move together; avoid attaching your ship to your harbor before assembly. The framework is suitable for custom pirate-ship builds, but very large builds and arbitrary modded blocks need individual testing. Back up the closed world before the first assembly. See the Eureka author's instructions.

Copy this pack off the work laptop

Copy Grand-Line-Odyssey-0.1.0-Windows.zip from the project's dist folder using a USB drive, cloud drive or your usual file transfer. You only need that ZIP. It contains the importable .mrpack and guides. It excludes development caches, accounts and test worlds. The finished bundle does not run a game or public server on this laptop.

On your gaming PC, extract the ZIP and open START-HERE.html. Import the inner .mrpack in Prism Launcher or Modrinth App. Sign into your own Minecraft Java account, use 64-bit Java 17, and let the launcher download the pinned files. Do not add the standalone custom JAR a second time; it is already in the pack.

Send the same ZIP and version to friends. They each import it and sign in with their own Java account. No save needs to be copied to joining players. The host's world is the shared world.

Play from different networks

Host on the gaming PC: open the world, then Esc → Open to LAN → Start LAN World. Wait for e4mc's public address in chat and share it privately with your crew. Friends use Multiplayer → Direct Connection and paste that address. This internet relay works beyond a home LAN; see e4mc.

Keep the host's Minecraft world open and PC awake. A closed world or sleeping PC ends the session; the next session may get a different address. If a relay address never appears, inspect logs/latest.log for e4mc/network errors. e4mc retains Minecraft's integrated server, so dedicated-server-only world-mod features are not enabled by it. Cross-network joining still needs a real test with two licensed accounts; this build has not claimed that test passed.

Move a played world later

Save and close the world first. Copy the entire saves/<world name> directory into the identical pack instance on the other PC. Keep its data, serverconfig, player data and ship-physics files together; copying only region files can lose progress or ships. Keep the original copy until the destination loads correctly. Use the same Java account to retain that character's story, rivals and inventory. Optional Ollama and its model are installed separately on whichever PC hosts.
