New in 0.5.1: city streets, faster sailing and crew rewards

City population now follows 64-block street quarters throughout each mapped island instead of stopping at a small number of neighborhoods. Wider roads beside concrete, quartz and terracotta buildings qualify, including the large Goa streets on Dawn Island. Existing towns and accepted errands keep their saved identities.

Small Ships vessels sail 60% faster by default, including existing Galleons and Cogs. Dropped items survive fire and lava, and town buildings are protected against supported combat damage while normal building remains allowed.

Every member who contributes to a quest earns the full credits, XP and ordinary equipment reward. Fruit items, fruit loot boxes and large ship items remain one shared crew payout, normally delivered to the member completing the hand-in. If that member already earned this quest reward, an unpaid contributor receives the shared item instead. Cargo is handed in only once. Open O → Voyage → Crew reward inbox to collect saved personal rewards after returning online. Existing completed quests are not paid again.

The journal also has a wider conversation reader, wrapped titles and messages, and separate controls for browsing quests and choosing actions. Resident dialogue includes faction reactions and errand updates that previously disappeared from the reading panel.

The chest-key and pipe recipe conflicts have targeted fixes. Three gold ingots in a vertical line craft the chest key. Both native pipes remain craftable with distinct recipes, described below.

Install or update

The full 0.5.1 pack includes 37 pinned downloads, including the four wildlife additions: Alex’s Mobs, Citadel, Upgrade Aquatic and Blueprint. Import the full pack when upgrading from before 0.4.0. Close Minecraft and back up the whole world. Import the 0.5.1 mrpack as a new instance, then copy your backed-up world folder into its saves folder. Keep your old instance as a fallback. Copy options.txt only if you want to preserve your custom controls. Everyone joining needs the same full pack. Open the download website in a normal browser.

One journal, existing progress

  • Story: your existing 83-chapter, four-path campaign system. Completed chapters stay complete. The current chapter lists destination coordinates, boss requirements, cargo and rewards. Meet your contact, start an encounter, or choose the chapter outcome here. A faction choice requires a second confirmation.
  • Town: neighborhood dialogue, local errands, resident services, relationship history and directions back to the original quest giver.
  • Voyage: the existing sailing, discovery, combat and delivery contracts, including your first ship and the galleon. The menu reads the same completion flags. The Crew reward inbox, after the contract list, holds personal rewards for returning contributors.
  • Training: opens Mine Mine no Mi's own existing training quest tracker. Its progression remains under that mod's control.

Click Track story, Track errand or Track contract to pin that objective on your screen. The tracker updates every two seconds. It shows the next requirement and coordinates where available. Hide tracker removes it; selecting Track brings it back. Scroll the reader with your mouse wheel or Page Up / Page Down; a visible scrollbar shows your place. Separate footer buttons move between pages. Tab and Enter work on menu buttons.

Right-click a resident or your story contact to open the appropriate journal tab. /journal, /story, /town and /voyage also open the menu. Existing detailed commands remain available, but accepting and finishing the supported quests no longer requires opening chat.

Read conversations and choose quests

On a compact game window, the text reader uses the full available width. Open Browse to choose a journal entry and Choices to see its available choices. Larger windows keep the quest list beside the reader and show a separate action area with page controls. Long titles, dialogue and action notices wrap instead of being squeezed into a single line; the reader displays complete text lines above its footer.

A resident's name, role and current activity appear above their conversation. The body separates the greeting, local rumor, reaction to your faction, any newly recorded errand progress, remembered help and the day's local event into short paragraphs. Choose a Quest: button to read an available job from that resident before accepting it. Opening those details does not accept or complete the quest; the existing contact, distance and objective requirements still apply.

The Crew reward inbox lists saved quest titles with their credits, XP and items before explaining the reward rules. Choose Collect saved rewards to receive them. Inventory space is checked first; anything that does not fit remains saved.

Town protection barriers

The host enables protectTownBlocks by default. On the One Mine Piece map, combat protection covers the full mapped island footprints, including towns and landmark buildings that nobody has visited yet. Saved neighborhoods and inhabited rooms also receive local protection in existing worlds. A short message above the hotbar tells you when you enter or leave the protected area.

The barrier protects blocks from explosions, supported Devil Fruit terrain destruction and transformations. Players can still mine, place blocks, open containers and fight enemies. It is an invisible protection rule, so it does not obstruct ships, roads or travel. Combat can still hurt players, enemies and residents; this is building protection.

Protection prevents new damage; it does not reconstruct buildings already destroyed. The host can turn it off with protectTownBlocks = false in config/grandline-towns.toml. The rule is enforced by the host for everyone in the world.

Dropped items survive fire

Dropped vanilla and modded items now ignore fire and lava damage. Pickup, ordinary despawning and other damage still work normally. This applies to item entities, not players, mobs or containers. Items already destroyed cannot be recovered.

Shared crew progress

The journal reads your actual Mine Mine no Mi crew membership. Its header shows the crew name when sharing is active. You do not need to create a second party.

  • On first use, the crew ledger imports the captain’s existing campaign and voyage completion. It does not reset the captain’s old save or merge incompatible personal campaigns.
  • Campaign destination visits and boss completions, sailing/combat totals, discovered/synchronized island sets, town errands and claimed voyage rewards are shared. Any member can contribute eligible progress.
  • A hand-in consumes cargo once and completes the quest for the crew. For delivery quests, each helper can choose Contribute supplies in the journal. This removes only the requested items still needed, saves them with the crew’s quest and records that member’s contribution. Partial deposits count. The final hand-in consumes only the missing supplies from the finishing member’s inventory.
  • Every eligible contributor earns the full credits and XP, plus their own ordinary equipment and clothing. The journal labels fruit items, fruit loot boxes and large ship items as shared: the member completing the hand-in normally receives that one crew payout, or an unpaid contributor receives it if the finisher already earned this quest reward.
  • Help with the quest objectives to qualify. A new member who has not contributed does not receive a reward just for joining. Offline contributors keep a saved personal reward in O → Voyage → Crew reward inbox, even if they later change crews. Each payout can be collected only once; leaving and rejoining does not reset it.
  • Already completed quests retain their completion flags and are not paid retroactively. The new distribution applies to rewards earned after updating.
  • The captain chooses the shared campaign and can abandon the shared town errand. Other members can accept available work, contribute and hand it in.
  • Selecting a tracked objective updates the crew’s tracked objective. Hiding the tracker is a personal display preference.
  • Crew quest state survives restarts, crew renames and captain transfers. A new member sees the existing crew ledger, including already claimed rewards.
  • Inventory, credits, fruit powers, native training quests, contact trust and town relationships remain personal. Leaving a crew restores your separate solo quest record; it does not erase the crew’s ledger.

Contribute to a delivery

Open the active delivery in Story, Town or Voyage and choose Contribute supplies. Story deposits require the current contact on the chapter’s island; town deposits require the correct resident in the original community. Voyage deposits use the contract’s journal entry. The server removes up to the remaining required amount from your inventory and records the donor, so one person can supply the planks while another supplies the wool. Dropping items to a friend does not by itself record a quest contribution.

The cargo total shows your inventory plus crew deposits. Deposited items stay assigned to that quest across restarts and are not charged again at completion. Contribute before someone finishes the quest. Helpers who disconnect keep their earned personal rewards. Only the captain can abandon a town errand; its deposited items return to their original donors through the reward inbox, without quest credits or XP.

Living neighborhoods

The update populates existing constructed streets across all 169 mapped destinations. It does not replace buildings, erase saves or force distant chunks to load. Visit a built-up area on foot. The system looks for dry, clear standing space beside construction; bare beaches, ocean and tree canopies do not qualify.

Residents arrive gradually: at most eight per visiting player per second, with at most two per street quarter in that pass and 16 per quarter by default. There is no lifetime neighborhood or room limit per island. A nearby crowd limit pauses new additions at 96 outdoor residents within 96 blocks; interiors have a separate 24-resident budget within 80 blocks, so street crowds cannot use the building allowance. These limits constrain new arrivals; they do not delete existing residents or guarantee a fixed total in overlapping player areas. Neighborhoods are shared by everyone in the world; inviting another player does not multiply the crowd. Their saved identities prevent duplicate spawning when chunks unload. Residents killed in the world leave a vacancy that can refill after twenty minutes of world time.

Each neighborhood has eight useful roles: Harbormaster, Cook, Shipwright, Medic, Courier, Lookout, Merchant and Chronicler, plus additional townspeople. They use original humanoid models with varied hair and work clothing. Most are original local residents; eight named residents are placed at three authored landmarks. See World life for exact coverage.

Residents walk to work during the day, gather later, and head to rest locations at night. Lookouts remain on watch. They seek their rest location when it rains and flee nearby monsters or injuries. These are navigation routines, not fully simulated households: if the existing streets have no accessible covered location, the system cannot create shelter for them. It does not add beds or change buildings.

Each role offers a practical service, from cooked meals and timber to medicine, maps, arrows and fishing equipment. Purchases use existing supply credits, with a one-credit discount at 20 local trust. Each service has five purchases per player per twenty-minute stock period.

Side stories and repeatable work

Each supported island has an original three-part relief story:

  1. Local relief: deliver the island's requested supply to its Medic. The briefing explains that neighborhood's problem.
  2. Three sides of the story: listen to the Cook, Shipwright and Lookout in order in the same neighborhood (or elsewhere on the same island for an errand begun inside a small building).
  3. Rebuilding: deliver repair materials to the Shipwright and decide whether to keep your payment or donate it.

There are also five repeatable errands per island: cooked-fish deliveries, replacement rigging, catching fish with a rod, defending the neighborhood and carrying messages between residents. This is 1,352 local quest instances across 169 destinations, built from eight shared quest patterns; it is not 1,352 different gameplay mechanics; 20 islands have authored briefings, and the remaining destinations share fallback community text.

Only catches and combat after accepting count. Fishing must take place on the relevant island. Watch targets must be hostile or actively attacking and within 96 blocks of the neighborhood; residents and summoned story bosses do not count. Messages must reach the specified roles in order. Accept and hand in within six blocks of the right resident. The journal gives the original neighborhood's coordinates.

One town errand can be active per crew (or solo player) at a time alongside your campaign and voyage contracts. Abandoning one pays no quest reward and returns each member’s deposited supplies through O → Voyage → Crew reward inbox. A full inventory leaves the refund safely waiting there. One-time story rewards cannot be repeated. Repeatable errands return twenty minutes after completion while the world runs; changing the daylight clock or dying does not reset that cooldown.

Crew members share the active errand, objective counts, completion and cooldowns. Players outside that crew have separate progress. Relationships remain personal. Helping increases town trust and existing local goodwill. Donating trades the crew’s credit payment for extra personal trust for the member completing the hand-in; contributors still receive the XP reward. Residents remember your last completed job and donations; attacking them reduces trust. This feeds the existing local-goodwill supply discounts rather than introducing a competing currency.

Pig spawning and loud explosions

This update includes three safeguards:

  • Invalid or missing Mine Mine no Mi custom-spawner entity IDs are blocked instead of silently falling back to pigs. Explicitly configured valid pig spawners remain supported.
  • Generated pig spawning is capped when twelve pigs are already within 32 blocks. Existing animals are not deleted. Breeding, spawn eggs and intentional command spawns are unaffected.
  • On updated clients, one-shot explosion and thunder effects are capped at 0.35 volume before the normal sound settings and limited to three starts per second, at least 150 milliseconds apart. Ordinary animal audio and other sound categories remain available. These audio limits also soften legitimate combat explosions.

The reported travel-triggered pig/explosion incident has not been reproduced in the player's world. The invalid-spawner fallback was verified in the pinned mod and guarded by a regression test, but it is not yet confirmed as that world's trigger. If the incident continues after every client updates, use /voyage diagnose in the affected area. It reports coordinates, nearby pig counts and invalid/custom pig spawners without deleting anything.

Population settings

The host can edit config/grandline-towns.toml with the game closed. Set residentsPerNeighborhood to 8 for lighter crowds, 16 for the default or up to 24 for denser streets. cityOutdoorResidentLimit controls nearby outdoor arrivals (default 96); cityIndoorResidentLimit controls indoor arrivals (default 24). The old neighborhoodsPerIsland, inhabitedRoomsPerIsland and nearbyResidentLimit settings are retained for compatibility but no longer restrict this city population system. Interior and wildlife details are described in World life. Lowering these limits affects future arrivals; it does not remove residents already saved. Setting enabled to false stops new population but leaves existing residents and errands available.

Verification and limits

The update is checked against the pinned Minecraft 1.20.1 / Forge 47.4.0 mod set. Tests cover quest spending, duplicate rewards, chain prerequisites, shared-world player isolation, saved NPC identity, death cooldowns, safe ground, relationship persistence, old campaign/voyage progress, journal action validation and spawn/audio safeguards. See Build checks for the final test record.

The update does not promise a rebuilt city on every island, unique cinematic animation, real household economies or an automatically generated unlimited quest story. Walkability depends on the existing map, and practical crowd density should be checked on your host hardware.
