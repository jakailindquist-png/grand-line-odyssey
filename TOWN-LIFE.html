Install or update

0.4.0 adds four third-party downloads: Alex’s Mobs, Citadel, Upgrade Aquatic and Blueprint. Replacing only the Odyssey JAR is not a complete update. Close Minecraft and back up the whole world. Import the 0.4.0 mrpack as a new instance, then copy your backed-up world folder into its saves folder. Keep your old instance as a fallback. Copy options.txt only if you want to preserve your custom controls. Everyone joining needs the same full pack. Open the download website in a normal browser.

One journal, existing progress

  • Story: your existing 76-chapter, four-path campaign system. Completed chapters stay complete. The current chapter lists destination coordinates, boss requirements, cargo and rewards. Meet your contact, start an encounter, or choose the chapter outcome here. A faction choice requires a second confirmation.
  • Town: neighborhood dialogue, local errands, resident services, relationship history and directions back to the original quest giver.
  • Voyage: the existing sailing, discovery, combat and delivery contracts, including your first ship and the galleon. The menu reads the same completion flags and awards the same rewards.
  • Training: opens Mine Mine no Mi's own existing training quest tracker. Its progression remains under that mod's control.

Click Track story, Track errand or Track contract to pin that objective on your screen. The tracker updates every two seconds. It shows the next requirement and coordinates where available. Hide tracker removes it; selecting Track brings it back. Scroll the details with your mouse wheel or Page Up / Page Down. The quest list has page buttons. Tab and Enter work on menu buttons.

Right-click a resident or your story contact to open the appropriate journal tab. /journal, /story, /town and /voyage also open the menu. Existing detailed commands remain available, but accepting and finishing the supported quests no longer requires opening chat.

Shared crew progress

The journal reads your actual Mine Mine no Mi crew membership. Its header shows the crew name when sharing is active. You do not need to create a second party.

  • On first use, the crew ledger imports the captain’s existing campaign and voyage completion. It does not reset the captain’s old save or merge incompatible personal campaigns.
  • Campaign destination visits and boss completions, sailing/combat totals, discovered/synchronized island sets, town errands and claimed voyage rewards are shared. Any member can contribute eligible progress.
  • A hand-in consumes items from the member who performs it and pays that member once. It marks the quest complete for the crew; another member cannot claim a second reward. Bring the delivery items together in one member’s inventory.
  • The captain chooses the shared campaign and can abandon the shared town errand. Other members can accept available work, contribute and hand it in.
  • Selecting a tracked objective updates the crew’s tracked objective. Hiding the tracker is a personal display preference.
  • Crew quest state survives restarts, crew renames and captain transfers. A new member sees the existing crew ledger, including already claimed rewards.
  • Inventory, credits, fruit powers, native training quests, contact trust and town relationships remain personal. Leaving a crew restores your separate solo quest record; it does not erase the crew’s ledger.

Living neighborhoods

The update populates existing constructed streets across all 169 mapped destinations. It does not replace buildings, erase saves or force distant chunks to load. Visit a built-up area on foot. The system looks for dry, clear standing space beside construction; bare beaches, ocean and tree canopies do not qualify.

Residents arrive gradually, two per neighborhood every ten seconds, up to 16 residents per neighborhood and 32 neighborhoods per island (older host config files may still say four) by default. Neighborhoods are shared by everyone in the world; inviting another player does not multiply the crowd. Their saved identities prevent duplicate spawning when chunks unload. Residents killed in the world leave a vacancy that can refill after twenty minutes of world time.

Each neighborhood has eight useful roles: Harbormaster, Cook, Shipwright, Medic, Courier, Lookout, Merchant and Chronicler, plus additional townspeople. They use original humanoid models with varied hair and work clothing. Most are original local residents; eight named residents are placed at three authored landmarks. See World life for exact coverage.

Residents walk to work during the day, gather later, and head to rest locations at night. Lookouts remain on watch. They seek their rest location when it rains and flee nearby monsters or injuries. These are navigation routines, not fully simulated households: if the existing streets have no accessible covered location, the system cannot create shelter for them. It does not add beds or change buildings.

Each role offers a practical service, from cooked meals and timber to medicine, maps, arrows and fishing equipment. Purchases use existing supply credits, with a one-credit discount at 20 local trust. Each service has five purchases per player per twenty-minute stock period.

Side stories and repeatable work

Each supported island has an original three-part relief story:

  1. Local relief: deliver the island's requested supply to its Medic. The briefing explains that neighborhood's problem.
  2. Three sides of the story: listen to the Cook, Shipwright and Lookout in order in the same neighborhood (or elsewhere on the same island for an errand begun inside a small building).
  3. Rebuilding: deliver repair materials to the Shipwright and decide whether to keep your payment or donate it.

There are also five repeatable errands per island: cooked-fish deliveries, replacement rigging, catching fish with a rod, defending the neighborhood and carrying messages between residents. This is 1,352 local quest instances across 169 destinations, built from eight shared quest patterns; it is not 1,352 different gameplay mechanics; 20 islands have authored briefings, and the remaining destinations share fallback community text.

Only catches and combat after accepting count. Fishing must take place on the relevant island. Watch targets must be hostile or actively attacking and within 96 blocks of the neighborhood; residents and summoned story bosses do not count. Messages must reach the specified roles in order. Accept and hand in within six blocks of the right resident. The journal gives the original neighborhood's coordinates.

One town errand can be active per crew (or solo player) at a time alongside your campaign and voyage contracts. Abandoning one pays nothing. One-time story rewards cannot be repeated. Repeatable errands return twenty minutes after completion while the world runs; changing the daylight clock or dying does not reset that cooldown.

Crew members share the active errand, objective counts, completion and cooldowns. Players outside that crew have separate progress. Relationships remain personal. Helping increases town trust and existing local goodwill. Donating trades the credit payment for extra trust. Residents remember your last completed job and donations; attacking them reduces trust. This feeds the existing local-goodwill supply discounts rather than introducing a competing currency.

Town conversations work offline and do not require an AI service; the existing optional AI story-contact feature remains available separately.

Pig spawning and loud explosions

This update includes three safeguards:

  • Invalid or missing Mine Mine no Mi custom-spawner entity IDs are blocked instead of silently falling back to pigs. Explicitly configured valid pig spawners remain supported.
  • Generated pig spawning is capped when twelve pigs are already within 32 blocks. Existing animals are not deleted. Breeding, spawn eggs and intentional command spawns are unaffected.
  • On updated clients, one-shot explosion and thunder effects are capped at 0.35 volume before the normal sound settings and limited to three starts per second, at least 150 milliseconds apart. Ordinary animal audio and other sound categories remain available. These audio limits also soften legitimate combat explosions.

The reported travel-triggered pig/explosion incident has not been reproduced in the player's world. The invalid-spawner fallback was verified in the pinned mod and guarded by a regression test, but it is not yet confirmed as that world's trigger. If the incident continues after every client updates, use /voyage diagnose in the affected area. It reports coordinates, nearby pig counts and invalid/custom pig spawners without deleting anything.

Population settings

The host can edit config/grandline-towns.toml with the game closed. Set residentsPerNeighborhood to 8 for lighter crowds, 16 for the default or up to 24 for denser streets. neighborhoodsPerIsland defaults to 32 for new configurations; existing configurations retain their saved value. Interior and wildlife limits are described in World life. Lowering these limits affects future arrivals; it does not remove residents already saved. Setting enabled to false stops new population but leaves existing residents and errands available.

Verification and limits

The update is checked against the pinned Minecraft 1.20.1 / Forge 47.4.0 mod set. Tests cover quest spending, duplicate rewards, chain prerequisites, shared-world player isolation, saved NPC identity, death cooldowns, safe ground, relationship persistence, old campaign/voyage progress, journal action validation and spawn/audio safeguards. See Build checks for the final test record.

The update does not promise a rebuilt city on every island, unique cinematic animation, real household economies or an automatically generated unlimited quest story. Walkability depends on the existing map, and practical crowd density should be checked on your host hardware.
